# Initial literature: cues, resources and judgments

Research checked 2026-09-10. These sources motivate tests, not a conclusion that
all visible class distinctions have disappeared.

| Source | What it contributes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [Bertrand and Kamenica, Coming Apart? Cultural Distances in the United States over Time](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24771), working paper 2018, published AEJ: Applied 2023 | Directly measures group distinguishability using consumer behavior, media, time use and attitudes; examines changes over time | Not a street-observation experiment. Broadly stable differences in many domains do not support a blanket global-flattening claim |
| [Oh, Shafir and Todorov, Economic status cues from clothes affect perceived competence from faces](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31819209/), 2020 | Clothing manipulations change competence impressions, demonstrating consequences of material cues | This is an impression effect, not proof that clothes reveal actual income or caste |
| [Kraus et al., Evidence for the reproduction of social class in brief speech](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1900500116), 2019 | Published observer research showing that brief speech can carry class information and influence hiring judgments | US social-class results cannot be transferred directly to Indian jati; speech adds information beyond a silent glance |
| [Han, Nunes and Drèze, Signaling Status with Luxury Goods: The Role of Brand Prominence](https://gwern.net/doc/culture/2010-han.pdf), 2010 | Distinguishes prominent and subtle luxury signals and audiences with different recognition | Common broad product categories need not imply identical signals to knowledgeable observers |
| [Mobarak and Rosenzweig, Selling Formal Insurance to the Informally Insured](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/121671/files/cdp1007.pdf), 2012 | Connects jati, informal insurance and economic shocks; motivates studying risk and consumption smoothing alongside income levels | A jati network's role is not captured by whether its members can be recognized visually |
| [Joshi, Kochhar and Rao, Fractal inequality in rural India](https://academic.oup.com/ooec/article/doi/10.1093/ooec/odab004/6520734), 2022 | Detailed jati and within-group economic differences in rural Bihar | A selected poor sample is not all India; measured overlap is not observer accuracy |
| [Telangana expert report, Volume II](https://des.telangana.gov.in/publications/SEEEPC/SEEEPC-IEWG%20Volume-2.pdf), released 2026 | Compares other socioeconomic dimensions among low-income respondents, useful for testing whether similar income means similar circumstances | Composite rankings and descriptive subgroup comparisons do not identify causal effects or actual street-level recognition |

[A worked smartphone-ownership example](phone-example.md) illustrates the
difference between a group gap and successful individual classification.

For current US adoption and household consumption, use primary sources:
[Pew Mobile Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/) and
[BLS Consumer Expenditure public-use microdata](https://www.bls.gov/cex/pumd.htm).
Track the release year, underlying fieldwork period, income definition, and
whether brand/model detail is observed. These sources are candidates; no new US
estimates have been produced in this repository.
